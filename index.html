Status and accuracy

Classifications were produced by an AI-assisted reading of each filing, and every label is tied to a verbatim excerpt verified against the filing’s own text. Human-level audit of the classifications is not yet complete: an evaluation against an independent reference labelling of a 50-record sample is under way, and no accuracy figure is reported here. Counts and labels should be checked against the filings before they are relied on.

Scope and selection

VA reports 20,984 document submissions on the proposed rule, comprising approximately 24,333 comments; the comment period closed on 3 September 2025, and all 20,984 submissions are posted to the public docket on regulations.gov. This index covers a defined subset of that record, selected by the following criteria.

First, near-duplicate submissions were consolidated (see Extraction and deduplication): of 11,486 submissions belonging to 214 template campaigns, 10,476 copies that differed from their template only in signature or contact details were withheld in favour of a single exemplar. 12 submissions with no text were also withheld.

Second, a remaining record is retained if it satisfies at least one of three conditions: (i) it is accompanied by an uploaded document; (ii) it was filed on behalf of an organization, as determined from the text of the filing rather than from the filer-entered organization field, which is unreliable; or (iii) VA’s final rule responds to it specifically (see VA responds to). A template exemplar is also retained where the only document-bearing copy of that template was consolidated. Comments entered only in the regulations.gov free-text field by private individuals are not included unless condition (iii) applies; a substantively reasoned comment entered in that field may therefore be absent.

Third, records reviewed manually and found to contain no argument directed at the rule (spam, off-topic material, or statements of stance without reasons) were excluded, each with a recorded reason; 27 records were excluded on this basis.

Extraction and deduplication

The docket was obtained as a bulk CSV export from regulations.gov, and attachments (255 files on 138 submissions) were retrieved from downloads.regulations.gov. Text was extracted with PyMuPDF for PDF files and python-docx for Word documents; scanned documents and images were processed by optical character recognition (easyocr), and OCR-derived text is flagged as such. Published third-party works attached as exhibits (reports, studies and articles) are represented by title only and were neither classified nor indexed for search.

Near-duplicate detection used MinHash locality-sensitive hashing over five-word shingles (128 permutations in 32 bands), with submissions clustered at an estimated Jaccard similarity of 0.55 and verified against exact similarity. A cluster member was consolidated into its exemplar only where it added no sentence, stance term or self-identifying statement (for example, military service) and retained at least 80% of the template text; members meeting any of those conditions were retained as separate records.

Classification

Organizational status was first determined for the 174 document-bearing or organization-signalled submissions by a large language model, with each determination supported by a quotation. Each retained letter was then read in full by a large language model (Anthropic Claude) against a fixed brief and a closed, controlled vocabulary: position on the rule (four values), filing type, commenter type and sub-type, evidence offered, commenter attributes, geographic scope, and 46 argument codes. Of those codes, 36 correspond to the headings under which VA organizes its response to comments in the final rule, nine to arguments identified in a manually reviewed sample of 50 comments that have no heading in VA’s response, and one is a residual category. The vocabularies were read from the database schema at run time and fixed by a reviewer before classification.

Each assigned value was required to be accompanied by a verbatim quotation from the source filing, and every quotation was verified programmatically against the extracted text, after normalizing Unicode, typographic quotation marks, whitespace, line-break hyphenation and footnote reference markers. Where any quotation in a record could not be located, all values for that record were discarded rather than partially retained; such records were re-processed once and, if still unverifiable, withheld for manual review. Fields not substantiated by the filing were left empty; an absent value should be read as “not established” rather than “not present”.

Manual corrections in the underlying database (for example, to commenter type) take precedence over model output, and were never overwritten by it. The classification is AI-assisted throughout; errors of omission and commission should be presumed to remain, and the labels are intended as a finding aid for identifying filings that warrant direct review, not as a citable characterization of any commenter. Individuals’ names are not displayed.

Arguments, strongest versions and VA’s response

VA’s final rule (90 FR 61310) responds to comments under a numbered outline of headings; each argument page sets VA’s response passage for that heading beside the filings coded to it. The grouping Arguments not in VA’s response outline describes the structure of VA’s document only, and does not establish whether VA considered the argument.

Strongest versions. For each argument and side, up to eight filings are shortlisted by evidence type (legal analysis, published citations, quantified figures, proposed regulatory text). Each is scored 0–2 on support, specificity, engagement with VA’s stated rationale (weighted double) and completeness, for a total of 0–10; for arguments outside VA’s outline, engagement is not scored and the total is 0–6. Filings scoring at least 80% of the maximum are grouped into up to four distinct lines of reasoning, which are ranked by pairwise comparison; a filing that is the only one on its side to raise an argument is shown only if it reaches that threshold. Each version carries a verified verbatim excerpt and the sentence of VA’s passage that best answers it, or a note that the passage does not. Beneath it, an assessment states in one sentence how far VA’s answer meets that version; it is made in this analysis, by the same model-assisted review that selects the versions, and is not VA’s text. That assessment is confined to the passage under the relevant heading; VA may address the point elsewhere in the rule. Selections are model-generated and have not yet been reviewed by a person.

VA responds to. Sentences in which VA describes a specific commenter or small number of commenters (“One commenter suggested …”; 34 instances across 16 headings) were extracted from the final rule. Candidate comments were retrieved by TF-IDF ranking over the full text of all 20,984 submissions, including attachments; a language model then identified the comment, if any, that makes the point described, with a verified supporting quotation; and each identification was confirmed manually. 23 of the 34 descriptions were matched.

Glossary
Comment letter
A comment filed on behalf of an organization, or any comment that included an uploaded document, rather than only text typed into the comment box.
Unique
One record per distinct comment; duplicate template copies are shown once.
Template campaign
A group of near-identical comments, the same letter sent by many people, found by comparing their text.
Counts are filings
Every number counts docket records, not people. A template letter is counted once, however many people sent it.
Position
The filing’s stance on the rule: Oppose, Support, Mixed (supports some parts, opposes others), or Neutral or technical. Blank when the filing states no stance.
Argument
A heading of VA’s response outline, or an argument outside it, that the filing makes, each backed by a quotation from the filing.
Commenter type
Organization or individual, judged from the filing itself. Sub-type gives the finer category.
Filing type
Substantive comment, or a brief general comment.
Evidence offered
What the filer put behind the argument: citations, legal analysis, case stories, figures, proposed regulatory text.
Strongest versions
The distinct lines of an argument that are hardest for VA’s response to set aside, selected as described above.
VA responds to
VA’s final rule answers this comment specifically.
Classification pending
The automated reading could not be traced word for word to the filing, so its labels were withheld; the filing is held for hand review.
